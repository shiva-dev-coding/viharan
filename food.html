Authentic Flavors of Bihar

Wholesome roasted sattu staples, smoky coal-fired classics, and GI-tagged festive sweets passed down through generations.

Signature Dish

Litti Chokha

Bihar's global culinary ambassador: whole-wheat dough balls stuffed with spiced roasted gram flour (sattu), baked over glowing cow-dung cakes or charcoal, dipped in pure desi ghee, and served with smoky mashed brinjal, tomato, and potato chokha.

Origin: Magadha & Bhojpur Taste: Smoky & Savory
GI-Tagged Sweet

Silao ka Khaja

Originating in Silao between Rajgir and Nalanda, this multi-layered, ultra-crispy puff pastry is fried to golden perfection and lightly glazed in cardamom-infused sugar syrup. Known for its delicate 52-layer crunch that melts in the mouth.

Origin: Silao, Nalanda Status: GI Tag Certified
Chhath Mahaprasad

Thekua

The sacred offering of Chhath Puja, crafted from whole-wheat flour, melted jaggery (gur), grated coconut, and fennel seeds, pressed onto intricately carved wooden molds and slow-fried in pure ghee into crunchy, aromatic cookies.

Origin: Pan-Bihar Festival: Chhath Puja
Protein-Rich

Sattu Paratha (Makuni)

Soft whole-wheat flatbreads generously stuffed with tangy roasted sattu mixed with finely chopped onions, green chilies, ginger, ajwain, and mustard oil pickle masala. Served hot with curd or baingan bharta.

Origin: Bihar & Purvanchal Key Ingredient: Roasted Chana Sattu
Natural Cooler

Sattu Sharbat

Bihar's legendary indigenous energy drink made by whisking roasted Bengal gram flour with chilled water, fresh lime juice, roasted cumin powder, black salt, mint leaves, and finely diced onions.

Season: Summer Superdrink Benefit: High Plant Protein
Makar Sankranti Classic

Dahi Chuda & Tilkut

A beloved wholesome breakfast and the centerpiece of Makar Sankranti celebrations—combining flattened rice (chuda) with thick creamy curd (dahi), organic jaggery, and Gaya's famous sesame-jaggery Tilkut.

Origin: Mithila & Magadha Festival: Makar Sankranti
Evening Street Classic

Bihari Chana Ghugni

Black chickpeas slow-cooked with caramelized onions, garlic, ginger, and aromatic garam masala, topped with crunchy sev, chopped coriander, green chilies, and a squeeze of fresh lemon—often paired with puffed rice (murhi) or chura.

Type: Spiced Snack Best With: Masala Chai & Murhi
Saturday Tradition

Bihari Masala Khichdi

A comforting Saturday staple across Bihari households—fragrant rice and lentils simmered with seasonal vegetables and tempered with cumin and ghee, traditionally served with "Char Yaar" (curd, papad, ghee, and spicy mango pickle/chokha).

Tradition: Saturday Feast Accompaniments: 4 Classic Sides
Mithila Delicacy

Sitamarhi ki Balushahi

Flaky on the outside and rich, juicy, and soft on the inside, Sitamarhi's signature Balushahi is crafted from fermented flour dough fried in pure desi ghee and soaked in saffron-cardamom syrup.

Origin: Sitamarhi, Mithila Texture: Flaky & Syrupy
Holi Special

Bihari Malpua

A rich festive pancake made with mashed ripe bananas, flour, milk, khoya, dry fruits, and crushed green cardamom, fried in ghee until crisp at the edges and dipped in fragrant sugar syrup.

Occasion: Holi & Weddings Secret Ingredient: Ripe Banana & Khoya
